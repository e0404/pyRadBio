# Katz cellular track-structure theory: Katz, Ackerson, Homayoonfar & Sharma 1971 (doi:10.2307/3573247)

*Inactivation of Cells by Heavy Ion Bombardment*, **Radiat. Res. 47**(2):402-425
(1971). The DOI has a JSTOR `10.2307/` prefix (not `10.1667/rr...`) and the paper
has four authors, so a bare "Katz et al. 1971" is ambiguous.

Implemented in `pyradbio/models/katz/model.py` as `KatzCellularModel`; tests
`tests/models/test_katz.py`.

## One-paragraph idea

The oldest model in the package and common ancestor of the LEM and MKM families.
Cell killing is attributed to the dose deposited by **delta rays** around the ion
path, so a gamma-ray dose-response curve plus a radial dose distribution predicts
heavy-ion survival. A cell is described by four fitted parameters (`sigma_0`,
`E_0`, `kappa`, `m`). Killing has two modes: *ion-kill* (a single traversing ion
kills outright) and *gamma-kill* (dose from several ions pools and kills through
the same multi-target statistic as gamma rays). `P` is both the single-ion kill
probability and the fraction of dose routed to ion-kill.

## Inputs

| symbol | meaning | unit | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sigma_0` | saturation ion-kill cross section | cm^2 | fitted, Figs. 6-15 |
| `E_0` | extrapolated gamma-ray D-37 dose | erg/cm^3 | fitted, Figs. 6-15 |
| `kappa` | track-structure parameter ("related to the size of the sensitive element") | - | fitted |
| `m` | gamma-ray extrapolation number | - | fitted |
| `Z`, `beta` | ion charge and relative speed | - | per bombardment |
| `L` | specific energy loss (LET) | MeV g^-1 cm^2 | per bombardment |
| `F` | fluence | cm^-2 | eq. (11) |

**Unit traps.** The paper is cgs. `1 Gy = 1e4 erg/cm^3` in water, so Chinese
hamster `E_0 = 1.95e4 erg/cm^3 = 1.95 Gy`; `1 cm^2 = 1e8 um^2`, so
`sigma_0 = 5.0e-7 cm^2 = 50 um^2` (the same value the LEM family later adopts for
V79). `k` is printed in rad^-1 (`1 Gy^-1 = 0.01 rad^-1`). Converting `E_0` and
`sigma_0` and checking they land on sensible Gy and um^2 confirms a column was read
correctly.

## Core equations (numbered as in the paper)

```
(3)   P      = (1 - e^{-E/E_0})^m                        m-target 1-hit site response
(4)   S      = int 2 pi t (1 - e^{-E/E_0})^m dt          ion-kill cross section
(5)   S/S_0  = (1 - e^{-z*^2/(kappa beta^2)})^m          grain-count approximation
(6)   E_0 beta^2 a_0^2 z*^-2 ~ 5e-8 erg/cm               grain-count -> track-width transition
(7)   E_0 a_0^2/kappa ~ 2e-7 erg/cm                      follows from (5)+(6)
(8)   P      = sigma/sigma_0 = (1 - e^{-z*^2/(kappa beta^2)})^m     THE central equation
(9)   N'     = N_0 e^{-sigma F}                          ion-kill survivors
(10)  N/N_0  = e^{-sigma F} (1 - [1 - e^{-(1-P)E/E_0}]^m)           full survival
(11)  E      = F L
(12)  z*     = Z [1 - e^{-125 Z^{-2/3} beta}]            Barkas effective charge
(13)  ln(N/N_0) = ln m - [sigma_0 P/L + (1-P)/E_0] E     high-dose expansion of (10)
(14)  k      = sigma_0 P/L + (1-P)/E_0                   extrapolated radiosensitivity
(15)  sigma_ext = k L
```

Eq. (10) is a product because the survivors of ion-kill are the initial population
for gamma-kill; only the `(1-P)` fraction of the dose acts in the gamma-kill
channel. Eq. (12) is `pyradbio.track.stopping_power.effective_charge`;
`KatzCellularModel.effective_charge` delegates to it.

## Known limits / failure modes

- **Only the grain-count regime is implemented.** Eq. (8) saturates at `sigma_0`;
  Katz reports `sigma/sigma_0 ~ 2` for argon on kidney cells (p. 419), the
  track-width regime, which needs eq. (4). The paper gives eq. (4)'s site-averaged
  dose `Ebar(t)` only as Figs. 2-3, computed numerically by the method of Butts &
  Katz 1967 (Radiat. Res. 30:855-71); no closed form is printed. Katz says cellular
  bombardments are "mostly in the grain-count regime", where the four-parameter
  fits are made.
- **`Ebar(t)` is a cylinder-averaged site dose, not a point radial dose.** The model
  is not a `pyradbio.track.base.RadialDoseModel` sibling and does not use `track/`.
  Paper conclusion (p. 408): sites with `t/a_0 < 1` see a dose going as
  `z*^2 beta^-2 a_0^-2`; sites with `t/a_0 > 2` see `z*^2 beta^-2 t^-2` (the `1/r^2`
  penumbra); the crossover is absorbed by `kappa`.
- **Not an LQ model, no `beta`.** The shoulder comes from the multi-target exponent
  `m`, so the low-dose gamma-kill term goes as `D^m` (`m = 2.5` for most lines). The
  exact `D -> 0` slope is the ion-kill term alone, near zero at low LET.
- **Sensitive-site structure is unknown:** `m` must not be read as a number of
  targets; eq. (3) is "a useful mathematical form", not a mechanism.
- **Ion and cell parameters are not separable:** no survival formula factors into an
  ion term times a cell term, so a quality factor built on LET alone is incomplete.

## Golden values for tests

Table I, Chinese hamster (`sigma_0 = 5.0e-7`, `E_0 = 1.95e4`, `kappa = 1400`,
`m = 2.5`), the paper's own **model line** (`th.` columns, never `exp.`):

| Z | beta | z*^2/beta^2 | L [MeV cm^2/g] | P | k (th.) [rad^-1] | sigma_ext (th.) [cm^2] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | .145 | 4.74e1 | 4.60e1 | 2.02e-4 | 5.26e-3 | 4.02e-9 (printed defect) |
| 2 | .145 | 1.90e2 | 1.91e2 | 5.70e-3 | 6.02e-3 | 1.84e-8 |

Reproduced to <= 1% (`tests/models/test_katz.py`).

**Table I defect:** the Z=1 `sigma_ext` is inconsistent with its own `k` and `L`.
Eq. (15) on `k = 5.26e-3 rad^-1`, `L = 46.0` gives `3.88e-9 cm^2`, not `4.02e-9`
(3.6% off); `4.02e-9` would need `L ~ 47.7`, while 46.0 is right for 10 MeV
protons, so the `sigma_ext` entry is the suspect. The Z=2 row is self-consistent
to 0.4% (`1.848e-8` vs `1.84e-8`); only it is used as a golden.

Table I's `L` is the paper's own stopping power: analytic Bethe gives 18.2 keV/um
for He at beta = 0.145 against the printed 19.1, so `k(LET)` curves built on Bethe
sit off Table I by that LET difference. Fed the paper's own (beta, L), the model
reproduces Table I.

## Implementation notes / gotchas

- **`alpha_beta()` reports eq. (14)'s `k`, with `beta = 0` exactly** (no quadratic
  coefficient exists). `k` is the *extrapolated (high-dose)* radiosensitivity, not an
  LQ initial slope; it is what Katz tabulates and compares with measured
  radiosensitivities, so it is the only defensible LQ analogue. Against a fitted LQ
  `alpha` it reads high wherever the curve has a shoulder. Use `survival()` for
  anything dose-resolved; do not rebuild a survival curve from these LQ parameters.
- **Mixed fields raise:** eq. (10) is a product of two non-LQ modes, so the
  dose-average mixing of `core/mixing.py` does not apply.
- **PDF text layer is unusable for numerals:** every display equation and all of
  Table I are images in the JSTOR scan. Read page images and zoom-crop figure
  panels (several are rotated 90 degrees).

## Companion paper and a typo in it

Katz, R., "Track Theory and Radiation Quality", IAEA-SM-145/9, in *Biophysical
Aspects of Radiation Quality* (IAEA, Vienna, 1971), pp. 11-23 (open access via
DigitalCommons@Nebraska). The pre-print companion (its ref. [3] is the Radiat. Res.
paper "to be published"); eqs. 1-9 map onto (3),(2),(3),(4),(5),(8),(9),(10),(14),
and its Table I tabulates all ten parameter sets, agreeing with the Radiat. Res.
figure panels except:

- **T-1 Kidney (Todd) `sigma_0` is printed `6.70x10^27 cm^2`** in the IAEA Table I.
  Impossible; Radiat. Res. Figs. 10 and 11 print `6.7x10^-7 cm^2` = 67 um^2, the
  only reading consistent with the column, the Barendsen T-1 pair (54 um^2) and a
  cell nucleus. The Radiat. Res. figure panels are the source of record.
